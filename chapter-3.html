LEVEL 1: CONCEPT CHECK

Same mean, different risk

Team A daily sales are 48, 49, 50, 51, 52. Team B sales are 30, 40, 50, 60, 70. Compare their means and ranges. What does the comparison show?

Identify the statistical concept, state the relevant formula or rule, calculate carefully, and finish with an interpretation in context.

Detailed solution

Result. Both means equal 50. Ranges are 52−48=452-48=4 and 70−30=4070-30=40. Interpretation. Equal averages do not imply equal stability: Team B is much more volatile. A measure of centre must therefore be accompanied by dispersion.

Boxplot and outliers

For an ordered dataset, Q1=18Q_1=18, median =24=24, Q3=30Q_3=30, minimum =10=10, maximum =55=55. Compute the IQR and Tukey fences. Is 55 a potential outlier?

Identify the statistical concept, state the relevant formula or rule, calculate carefully, and finish with an interpretation in context.

Detailed solution

Result. IQR=30−18=12IQR=30-18=12. Lower fence =18−1.5(12)=0=18-1.5(12)=0; upper fence =30+1.5(12)=48=30+1.5(12)=48. Since 55>4855>48, 55 is a potential upper outlier. Interpretation. “Potential” means the observation deserves investigation; it should not be deleted automatically.

LEVEL 3: APPLICATION

Comparing relative dispersion

Store A has mean weekly sales EUR 20,000 and SD EUR 3,000. Store B has mean EUR 8,000 and SD EUR 1,600. Compare absolute and relative variability.

Identify the statistical concept, state the relevant formula or rule, calculate carefully, and finish with an interpretation in context.

Detailed solution

Method. Use SD for absolute spread and CV=s/x‾CV=s/\bar x for relative spread when means are positive and ratio-scale comparisons are meaningful. Result. A has the larger SD (3,000 versus 1,600). But CVA=15%CV_A=15\% and CVB=20%CV_B=20\%. Interpretation. Store B is less variable in euros but more variable relative to its typical sales level.

LEVEL 4: SYNTHESIS

Lorenz curve and Gini coefficient

Five households have incomes 10, 10, 20, 20 and 40. Construct cumulative population and income shares, then calculate the Gini coefficient using trapezoids.

Identify the statistical concept, state the relevant formula or rule, calculate carefully, and finish with an interpretation in context.

Detailed solution

Method. Total income is 100. Population shares are X=(0,.2,.4,.6,.8,1)X=(0,.2,.4,.6,.8,1) and cumulative income shares are Y=(0,.1,.2,.4,.6,1)Y=(0,.1,.2,.4,.6,1). The area under the Lorenz curve is A=∑(Yi−1+Yi)(Xi−Xi−1)/2A=\sum (Y_{i-1}+Y_i)(X_i-X_{i-1})/2. Result. The five trapezoids have areas .01, .03, .06, .10 and .16, so A=.36A=.36 and G=1−2A=.28G=1-2A=.28. Interpretation. Inequality is positive but not extreme. The Gini summarises the whole distribution and should be reported with context or the Lorenz curve.
