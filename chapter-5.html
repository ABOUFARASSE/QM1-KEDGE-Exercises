Components and models

For each description identify trend, seasonality, cycle or irregular component: long-run e-commerce growth; December sales peaks; recession-related movements; a one-day system failure. When is a multiplicative model preferable?

Identify the statistical concept, state the relevant formula or rule, calculate carefully, and finish with an interpretation in context.

Detailed solution

Result. Long-run growth is trend; recurring December peaks are seasonality; recession movements are cyclical; the failure is irregular. A multiplicative model is preferable when seasonal amplitudes rise with the series level; an additive model suits roughly constant absolute seasonal effects.

LEVEL 2: GUIDED CALCULATION

Moving averages

Monthly demand for five months is 90, 105, 99, 120, 126. Calculate the three-period centred moving averages for months 2, 3 and 4.

Identify the statistical concept, state the relevant formula or rule, calculate carefully, and finish with an interpretation in context.

Detailed solution

Method. Average each consecutive window of three observations and attach it to the middle month. Result. MA2=(90+105+99)/3=98MA_2=(90+105+99)/3=98; MA3=(105+99+120)/3=108MA_3=(105+99+120)/3=108; MA4=(99+120+126)/3=115MA_4=(99+120+126)/3=115. Interpretation. Smoothing reveals an upward underlying movement while reducing month-to-month noise; endpoints are lost because a centred window requires neighbours.

Identify the statistical concept, state the relevant formula or rule, calculate carefully, and finish with an interpretation in context.

Detailed solution

Method. t‾=2.5\bar t=2.5, Y‾=59\bar Y=59. ∑(t−t‾)(Y−Y‾)=23\sum(t-\bar t)(Y-\bar Y)=23 and ∑(t−t‾)2=5\sum(t-\bar t)^2=5, so b=4.6b=4.6 and a=59−4.6(2.5)=47.5a=59-4.6(2.5)=47.5. Result. Ŷt=47.5+4.6t\hat Y_t=47.5+4.6t and Ŷ5=70.5\hat Y_5=70.5. Interpretation. The fitted trend increases by 4.6 units per period; the forecast assumes no structural break.

Multiplicative seasonal forecast

Quarterly trend is Tt=100+3tT_t=100+3t. Seasonal factors are .80, 1.05, .95 and 1.20 for Q1–Q4.

  1. Check normalisation.

  2. Forecast t=9,10,11,12t=9,10,11,12.

  3. Interpret the Q4 factor.

Identify the statistical concept, state the relevant formula or rule, calculate carefully, and finish with an interpretation in context.

Detailed solution

Method. The factors should average 1: (.80+1.05+.95+1.20)/4=1(.80+1.05+.95+1.20)/4=1. Compute TtT_t then multiply by the relevant seasonal factor. Result. Trends are 127, 130, 133 and 136. Forecasts are 101.6, 136.5, 126.35 and 163.2. Interpretation. Q4 is expected to be 20% above trend, not 20 percentage points above the previous quarter.

LEVEL 4: SYNTHESIS

Forecast evaluation

Actual values are 100, 120, 110, 130. Model A forecasts 98, 118, 115, 128; Model B forecasts 105, 112, 108, 140. Calculate MAE for both and recommend a model. What else should be checked?

Identify the statistical concept, state the relevant formula or rule, calculate carefully, and finish with an interpretation in context.

Detailed solution

Result. Absolute errors for A are 2, 2, 5, 2, so MAEA=2.75MAE_A=2.75. For B they are 5, 8, 2, 10, so MAEB=6.25MAE_B=6.25. Model A performs better by MAE. Interpretation. The comparison should use a genuine validation period and also inspect bias, scale-sensitive versus percentage errors, residual patterns and performance after structural breaks.
