LEVEL 1: CONCEPT CHECK

Reading a relationship

A scatter plot of advertising expenditure and sales slopes upward, with points close to a line except for one distant observation.

  1. Describe direction, form and strength.

  2. What should be investigated about the distant point?

  3. Does the plot prove advertising causes sales?

Identify the statistical concept, state the relevant formula or rule, calculate carefully, and finish with an interpretation in context.

Detailed solution

Result. The direction is positive, the form is approximately linear and the association appears strong. The distant point may be a data error, an unusual campaign or an influential observation; verify it and compare results with and without it. Interpretation. The plot does not prove causality because seasonality, product quality or reverse causality may affect both variables.

LEVEL 3: INTERPRETATION

$R^2$, interpolation and extrapolation

Suppose a model fitted for advertising budgets from EUR 1,000 to EUR 10,000 has R2=.81R^2=.81. Interpret R2R^2. Classify predictions at EUR 7,000 and EUR 20,000 and explain the risk.

Identify the statistical concept, state the relevant formula or rule, calculate carefully, and finish with an interpretation in context.

Detailed solution

Result. The model explains 81% of the observed variation in sales around their mean; it does not mean that advertising causes 81% of sales. EUR 7,000 is interpolation, while EUR 20,000 is extrapolation. Interpretation. Extrapolation is risky because the linear relationship may not continue beyond the data and market saturation may appear.

LEVEL 4: SYNTHESIS

From association to a decision

A retailer finds r=.85r=.85 between discounts and weekly sales and recommends permanent large discounts. Give a statistically responsible response including at least four checks.

Identify the statistical concept, state the relevant formula or rule, calculate carefully, and finish with an interpretation in context.

Detailed solution

Result. Check the scatter plot for non-linearity and influential points; inspect residuals; control for holidays, advertising and store differences; examine whether discounting was targeted to weak weeks; distinguish revenue from profit; and, where feasible, use a randomised or credible quasi-experimental design. Interpretation. Strong correlation supports prediction inside the observed setting but is insufficient for a pricing policy because causal effects and margins determine profitability.
