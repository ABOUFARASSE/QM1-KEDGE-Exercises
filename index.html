LEVEL 1 · RECOGNITION

Statistical vocabulary

A survey asks 240 first-year students about programme, transport mode, satisfaction (1–5), number of absences and commuting time in minutes.

  1. Identify the population, statistical unit and five variables.
  2. Classify every variable as nominal, ordinal, discrete or continuous.
  3. Give one possible value for each variable.

Classify a variable from the meaning of its values, not from the way it is coded.

Detailed solution

Method. The population is the complete group addressed by the question; the statistical unit is one entity on which observations are recorded.

Answer. The population is the 240 surveyed students. The unit is one student. Programme and transport mode are nominal. Satisfaction is ordinal. Absences are discrete. Commuting time is continuous.

Interpretation. A number used as a code does not automatically make a variable quantitative.

LEVEL 2 · GUIDED REASONING

Population, sample and bias

KEDGE wants the average commuting time of all 1,200 first-year students. An online questionnaire is posted at 8 a.m.; 180 volunteers answer before noon.

  1. Define the target population and sample.
  2. Identify two plausible biases.
  3. Propose a better sampling design.

A large sample is not automatically representative.

Detailed solution

Method. Separate the target group from the observed group.

Answer. The target population is all 1,200 students; the sample is the 180 volunteers. Voluntary response and the morning window can create bias. Draw a random sample from the full list, possibly stratified by programme, and analyse non-response.

LEVEL 4 · SYNTHESIS

Audit a misleading dashboard

A manager says satisfaction “doubled” because a truncated-axis chart rises from 3.8 to 4.2, using answers from one student association.

List four problems, redesign the communication and write a defensible conclusion.

Audit the numerical claim, graph scale, sampling frame and scope of inference.

Detailed solution

The increase is (4.2/3.8 − 1) × 100 = 10.53%, not 100%. The truncated axis exaggerates it; the convenience sample is not representative; and the ordinal distribution should accompany the average. Use a 1–5 scale, show percentages, disclose n and recruitment. Conclude only for responding association members.
